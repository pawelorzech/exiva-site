7.2. When the right of withdrawal ceases — read this carefully

First a correction, because a widespread simplification here works against consumers. The exception usually invoked to justify "no refunds for digital content" is Art. 38(1)(13) of the Consumer Rights Act (numbering as amended by Journal of Laws 2022 item 2337, in force from 1 January 2023; previously Art. 38(13)). It reads:

"(13) for the supply of digital content not supplied on a tangible medium, for which the consumer is obliged to pay a price, if the trader began performance with the consumer's express and prior consent, the consumer having been informed before performance began that they would lose the right of withdrawal once the trader had performed, and having acknowledged this, and the trader having provided the consumer with the confirmation referred to in Art. 15(1) and (2) or Art. 21(1)."

The conditions are cumulative — miss one and the right of withdrawal survives in full:

  1. the subject matter is digital content not supplied on a tangible medium, for which you pay;
  2. the trader began performance;
  3. this happened with your express and prior consent;
  4. before performance began you were informed that you would lose the right of withdrawal and acknowledged it (the information duty is in Art. 12(1)(12));
  5. the trader provided confirmation of the contract on a durable medium — for distance contracts that is Art. 21(1), and the confirmation must also cover "information about the consumer's consent to the supply of digital content in circumstances causing the loss of the right of withdrawal" (Art. 21(1)(2)).

Why that exception probably does not apply to us at all. A Premium subscription is not one-off digital content — it is a digital service supplied continuously. The Act distinguishes "digital content" (Art. 2(5)) from "digital service" (Art. 2(5a)) and uses the pair consistently, yet Art. 38(1)(13) mentions digital content only. Services fall under Art. 38(1)(1), which requires the trader to have "fully performed the service" — which, for a renewing service, cannot happen within 14 days.

The conclusion we adopt, in your favour: on an Exiva Premium subscription you keep the 14-day right of withdrawal. We do not invoke Art. 38(1)(13) and we do not ask you to waive anything.

What we do instead. If you want Premium immediately, we take your express request that performance begin before the withdrawal period ends, under Art. 21(2) of the Act. On the purchase screen, before payment is confirmed, we show a separate, not pre-ticked checkbox:

☐ I request that the Exiva Premium service begin immediately after purchase, before the 14-day withdrawal period expires. I acknowledge that if I withdraw after performance has begun, I will pay for what was performed up to the moment of withdrawal, in proportion to the period I used the service (Art. 35 of the Consumer Rights Act). I keep my right of withdrawal for the full 14 days.

Ticking it is voluntary and is recorded with the date and time.

If you do not tick it, Premium features are activated after 14 days from purchase, and during that time you keep the right of withdrawal at no cost.

If you tick it and then withdraw, we refund the amount paid, reduced in proportion to the period you actually used the service, under Art. 35(1) and (2). We do not keep the whole fee and we do not refuse the refund.

Purchase record. Google Play provides the purchase confirmation and order record. Exiva does not receive an account email and does not claim to send a separate purchase confirmation. You may save a copy of these Terms and contact us using the address in section 1.

If we fail any of these duties, Art. 36(1) of the Act provides that you bear no cost for what was performed up to withdrawal. We write that here so there is no doubt on whose side the risk of our mistake lies.
